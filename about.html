02 The story behind the work

I came to Chennai in 2004 and worked in a few jobs before I found my way into the knowledge industry. I freelanced for a while, then went out on my own, and in 2008 I started a training company.

In 2017 a client put a chequebook on the table and asked me to coach his business full time. I have been coaching ever since. I did not decide to become a business coach. The market made me one, and I have been working since then to become the best one in the industry.

I started my first business at 19. There was no business family behind me and there was no safety net under me. I have made payroll in months when the money was not there, and I know what that pressure does to a founder because I have felt it myself.

Then I added the credentials. An MBA in International Trade and Finance, so that I understand how a business earns and where its money goes. An M.Sc in Applied Psychology with 2 years of clinical training under practising psychologists, so that I understand what is happening inside you while you run the business.

Most coaches understand businesses. Very few of them understand the founder who is running the business. I do both, and most coaches stop at one of the 4 things in the next section.

I have sat where you are sitting.

The MBA in International Trade and Finance means that I understand how the business earns and where the money goes, without you having to explain it to me. The M.Sc in Applied Psychology, with 2 years of clinical training behind it, means that I can read you. And I have been an entrepreneur since I was 19, which means I have sat in the chair you are sitting in and I have made the expensive mistakes myself.

Most coaches have one of those. The advice from a coach who has one of them is usually correct, and it usually does not stick. That is not a failure of effort on anybody's part. It is structural, because the person giving the advice could only see part of the picture.

04 What I believe

3 things I will argue about.

These are not values. Values are what a company says it admires. These 3 will lose me a certain kind of client, and that is what they are for.

One

Work ethic builds businesses, and intellect does not.

Smart people with poor execution stay stuck. Average people with exceptional consistency build extraordinary businesses. I have watched this happen too many times to call it a coincidence.

Two

The market owes you nothing.

It has every right to say no, and it will say no as many times as it wants to. Your job is to understand why it is saying no, and to keep fixing the answer until it says yes.

Three

There is no bad luck in business, only bad decisions.

Business success may carry a touch of luck. Business failure does not. Accepting that is uncomfortable for about a week, and it is the most liberating thing a founder can do.

07 The methodology

There are 6 domains, and everything I do sits in one of them.

I have built proprietary frameworks across 6 domains over 9 years inside real businesses. The ones I use most are written down in the document at the top of this page, because a method you cannot inspect is not a method. It is a claim.

  • 01DiagnosisI find the real problem sitting upstream of the one you present, and I do not prescribe anything until something has been measured.
  • 02ExecutionI turn intent into output on a rhythm. This is where good strategy quietly dies, and it is where consistency beats intellect every single time.
  • 03Revenue ArchitectureMost businesses run one way of making money when they have access to 4 or 5. This domain is the gap between those 2 sentences.
  • 04SalesI turn the founder from the company's best salesperson into the person who designs how the company sells.
  • 05MarketingI turn spending that nobody can measure into something written down that you can repeat. We build the thinking first, then the process, then the whole of it.
  • 06People and LeadershipThe team you have is the team you built, whether or not you meant to build it that way. This domain makes it deliberate.
